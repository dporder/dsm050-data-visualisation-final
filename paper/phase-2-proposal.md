# A second study with the makers

The repository study leaves basic questions about makers unresolved. Their
occupations, intentions and audiences can only be inferred from public traces.
A follow-up survey and interview study would ask them directly and examine how
those accounts relate to what they built.

## Study design

Recruitment would take place through the author's community and partner
networks. The survey would ask about prior programming experience, occupation,
the purpose of a recent project, its intended audience and whether it still
runs. It would also ask participants to share a project link voluntarily.
These linked records would allow the public-data indicators to be compared
with information supplied by the maker.

The initial target is several hundred survey responses. Fifteen to twenty-five
interviews would then be selected across different experience levels. Interviews
would explore decisions that a fixed survey answer can miss, including how a
participant's understanding of code changed during a project.

Consented observations at community events could provide a further check on
what was made and whether it still worked a month later. These would be repeated
observations with known dates, addressing a limit of the present liveness snapshot.

## What this would add

Direct accounts of experience would allow occupation-related proxies to be
evaluated. Participants could also describe motivation, audience and reasons
for abandoning a project. Repeated checks would make it possible to study
changes in availability, with uncertainty about unobserved intervals retained.

## Ethics and recruitment

The study would require university ethics review, an information sheet, informed
consent and a clear withdrawal procedure. The author's role in the recruiting
community creates a conflict that must be stated and managed. Participation
would be voluntary and separate from access to community activities.

Community membership figures and claims about participants' prior coding
experience would need documented evidence before use in the study. The survey
would provide an opportunity to measure those characteristics directly.
