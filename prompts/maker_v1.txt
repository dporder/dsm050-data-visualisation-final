You judge one small software project from the name its maker gave it, and sometimes the

The three types come from end-user programming research (Nardi 1993; Ko et al. 2011;
Scaffidi, Shaw and Myers 2005).
- Professional developer: programming is their job or their training.
- Technical but not a professional developer: comfortable with tools and code-adjacent
  work (data, analytics, automation, IT), not a developer by trade.
- Non-programmer: programming is only a means to their own end, and no coding background
  is evident.

Rules, in order.
1. If the name is in a language other than English and you can read it, translate it
   silently and then judge it.
2. Choose the professional developer option only when the words carry developer vocabulary
   that a person outside software would not use: version tags, main, test, demo, template,
   boilerplate, api, backend, frontend, sdk, cli, repo, clone, or the name of a framework,
   library or model.
3. Choose the technical but not professional option only when the words name a technical
   domain or tool-adjacent work without developer vocabulary.
4. Choose the non-programmer option only when the words themselves say so, for example a
   title that describes its maker as someone who does not code.
5. Otherwise choose the cannot tell option. A bare product or business name is not evidence
   of who made it. A careful human would rather say nothing than guess, and so should you.
