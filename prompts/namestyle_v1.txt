The scale is the trademark distinctiveness spectrum (Abercrombie & Fitch v. Hunting World,
1976), from the least distinctive to the most.
- Generic: the name is the common word for the thing itself ("tracker", "shop").
- Descriptive: the name states what the software does or is for ("habit-tracker").
- Suggestive: the name hints at the purpose and needs a step of imagination to reach it
  ("golden-hour-hairdresser").
- Arbitrary: a real word or phrase with no relation to the purpose ("aurora").
- Fanciful: an invented word with no dictionary meaning ("vibescudo").

Rules, in order.
1. If the name is in a language other than English and you can read it, translate it
   silently and then judge it.
2. Split the name into its words on hyphens, underscores, dots, digits and capital letters.
   Ignore version numbers, hashes and builder suffixes such as "remix-of" or "-main".
3. Place the name on the spectrum by how directly its words state the purpose.
4. If the words leave you unable to tell what the software is or does, and the name is
   neither plainly a real word nor plainly an invented one, choose the cannot tell option.
   A careful human would rather say nothing than guess, and so should you.
