The scale is the trademark distinctiveness spectrum (Abercrombie & Fitch v. Hunting World,
1976), from the least distinctive to the most.
- Generic: the name is the common word for the thing itself ("tracker", "shop").
- Descriptive: the name states what the software does or is for ("habit-tracker").
- Suggestive: the name hints at the purpose and needs a step of imagination to reach it
  ("golden-hour-hairdresser").
- Arbitrary: a real word or phrase with no relation to the purpose ("aurora").
- Fanciful: an invented word with no dictionary meaning ("vibescudo").

Rules, in order.
1. Split the name into its words on hyphens, underscores, dots, digits and capital letters.
   Ignore version numbers, hashes, a redacted owner and builder suffixes such as "remix-of".
2. Translate words from other languages, including transliterated Arabic, Urdu, Hindi or
   Malay words you recognise, and expand standard abbreviations before judging: "reg" is
   registration, "dash" is a dashboard, "cab" is a taxi, "viz" is visualisation, "cardapio" is
   a menu, "koora" is football, "ar-condicionado" is air conditioning. A standard abbreviation
   counts as a plain function word. A pun or portmanteau that has to be decoded (a name that
   fuses "generate" and "vision") is not an abbreviation; it is wordplay, and wordplay is
   Suggestive.
3. Descriptive when any word plainly names the software's function, artefact, audience or
   domain: tracker, chat, website, portfolio, menu, dashboard, toolkit, record keeper, exam
   preparation, jobs hub, seniors connect, door app, football live. A brand, place, person's
   name, decorative word or unreadable fragment beside it does not stop the name being
   descriptive: Descriptive beats Suggestive and Cannot tell whenever one plain function word
   or standard abbreviation is present anywhere in the name. Connective words on their own
   (connect, global, hub, flow, link, network) are not function words: with a named audience
   ("seniors connect") they are descriptive, with only a brand or place beside them the name is
   Suggestive.
4. Suggestive when the purpose is reached only through a metaphor, wordplay or an evocative
   word (journey, wizard, scribe, launchpad, frontier, seascapes, a strategy of life), a
   translated metaphor, or a truncated word that hints at a domain ("catho" for Catholic,
   "uni" for university), with no plain function word beside it.
5. Generic when the whole name, after translation, is nothing but the common noun for the
   product or service category itself (air conditioning, tracker, shop), with no other word.
   Such a name is Generic, not Descriptive.
6. Fanciful only when the whole name is one coined word with no dictionary word inside it.
7. Arbitrary only when the page title tells you the purpose and the name is a real word
   unrelated to it. When the purpose is unknown, a real word or phrase is Cannot tell, not
   Arbitrary.
8. Cannot tell for names whose purpose cannot be placed: hackathon, challenge, test or demo
   names; adjective-plus-noun builder combinations with no function word ("straightforward
   import", "visual app prototype"); "clone" names; fragments or acronyms run together; and real
   words or phrases whose relation to the software is unknown.
